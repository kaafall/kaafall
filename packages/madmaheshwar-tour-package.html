Madmaheshwar Tour Guide: Price, Opening Date, Best Time & How to Reach

Madmaheshwar, also spelled Madhmaheshwar and Madhyamaheshwar, is the second of the five Panch Kedar temples (Dwitiya Kedar) in Rudraprayag district, Uttarakhand, at 3,497 m (11,473 ft). The stone temple, where the navel (nabhi) of Lord Shiva is worshipped, has no motorable road: you walk about 16 km one way from Ransi village via Gaundar, Bantoli, Khatara and Nanu. This guide covers package price, 2027 opening dates, best time, route, difficulty and how to reach from your city.

Madmaheshwar Temple Opening & Closing Date 2027

Madmaheshwar is open for roughly six months a year. In 2026 the doors opened on 21 May and closed around 19–20 November. The opening date is announced at Omkareshwar Temple, Ukhimath, around Mahashivratri, and the closing date is fixed on Vijayadashami. Two days before opening, the deity's palanquin (doli) leaves Ukhimath and halts at Ransi and Gaundar on its way to the temple. Expect the 2027 season to begin around the third week of May; we confirm the exact dates before you book.

Best Time to Visit Madmaheshwar

Late May to June has the temple open, pleasant weather and green meadows. September to October gives the clearest Chaukhamba views after the monsoon, with colder nights. July and August are monsoon months with slippery trails and landslide risk, so we avoid them. November is very cold and the temple closes in the second half of the month; from November to April the trail is under snow and the deity is worshipped at Omkareshwar Temple in Ukhimath.

Madmaheshwar Trek Distance, Route & Difficulty

The trek is about 16 km one way (32 km round trip) from Ransi, at roughly 1,980 m, up to the temple at 3,497 m. The route runs Ransi → Gaundar (about 6 km, gentle) → Bantoli (about 10 km from Ransi) → Khatara → Nanu → Madmaheshwar. Difficulty is moderate: the first day follows the river valley, while the toughest stretch is the steep, continuous climb between Bantoli and Khatara. Walk 4–5 km daily with a loaded bag for a few weeks before you travel, and start every trek day early.

Budha Madmaheshwar

Budha Madmaheshwar is a small meadow and lake about 2 km above the temple. The climb is short but steep, and the reward is a 360° view of Chaukhamba, Kedarnath and Neelkanth on clear mornings. We plan it on the rest day with an early start, so you are back at the temple by late morning.

Madmaheshwar in the Panch Kedar Yatra

The Panch Kedar are Kedarnath (hump), Madmaheshwar (navel), Tungnath (arms), Rudranath (face) and Kalpeshwar (hair). According to legend, the Pandavas sought Lord Shiva after the Kurukshetra war and he took the form of a bull, whose body parts surfaced at these five places. Stays, Food & Network on the Madmaheshwar Trail

Stays are simple homestays and lodges at Ransi, Gaundar, Bantoli and near the temple, with shared bathrooms in places and hot vegetarian meals. There is no electricity near the temple, so carry a power bank. Mobile network works at Ukhimath and Ransi but is mostly absent beyond, and there is no ATM on the trail, so carry cash. Porters and mules can be arranged at Ransi at extra cost (porter included in Premium).

How to Reach Madmaheshwar From Delhi, Mumbai & Other Cities

By train: Take a train to Haridwar or Rishikesh (Yog Nagari) from Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Bengaluru or any major city; we pick you up from the station. By air: Fly to Jolly Grant Airport, Dehradun (flights from Delhi and several other metros); Rishikesh is about 20 km away. By road from Delhi: Delhi → Haridwar → Rishikesh → Devprayag → Srinagar → Rudraprayag → Ukhimath → Ransi, about 450 km, then trek about 16 km to the temple. Because of the distance, we recommend an overnight halt at Ukhimath.

Why Choose a 5N/6D Madmaheshwar Plan?

Many packages squeeze Madmaheshwar into 2N/3D or 3N/4D, which means long back-to-back drives and a very tight 32 km round trip at altitude. Our 5N/6D plan from Rishikesh adds an Ukhimath halt, a gentler first trek day and a rest day for Budha Madmaheshwar. If you have fewer days, ask us for a shorter custom plan.
